How to measure your ring size at home

You need a strip of paper or string, a ruler with millimetres, and five minutes in the evening. Here are the two reliable methods and a size table.

The sizes themselves

Ring sizes are just the inner circumference of the ring. The EU size is that circumference in millimetres: an EU 54 is a ring 54 mm around the inside. US sizes are a numbered scale that rises by about 2.55 mm of circumference per whole size. The UK uses letters. So whatever you measure, convert it to millimetres of circumference and the rest is a lookup.

Method 1: string and ruler

  1. Cut a thin strip of paper about 5 mm wide, or use a piece of string that does not stretch.
  2. Wrap it snugly around the base of the finger, over the knuckle if the knuckle is the widest point, so that the ring will actually pass over it.
  3. Mark where the strip overlaps with a pen.
  4. Lay the strip flat and measure from the end to the mark in millimetres. That number is your EU size.
  5. Repeat twice and take the average; a 1 mm difference is a third of a size.

Method 2: a ring you already own

Take a ring that fits the right finger well. Measure its inner diameter in millimetres with a ruler, edge to edge across the widest point of the hole, and multiply by 3.14 to get the circumference. A ring with an inner diameter of 17.2 mm is 17.2 x 3.14 = 54 mm around, an EU 54, about a US 6.75. Printed ring-size charts also work: lay the ring over the circles until the inner edge matches one exactly.

In short

  • EU size = inner circumference in mm. Measure that and look it up.
  • Measure the finger in the evening, at room temperature, and over the knuckle.
  • Wide bands feel tighter: go half a size up for 6 mm and more.

When to measure and common mistakes

Fingers swell in heat and after exercise and shrink in the cold, so measure in the evening at room temperature, when they are at their everyday largest. Measure the actual finger: left and right hands differ, often by half a size. Do not pull the string tight enough to dent the skin; the ring should slide over the knuckle with slight resistance and then sit comfortably. And remember width: a band 6 mm wide or more covers more skin and feels tighter than a slim one, so go up half a size.

If it is still wrong

Plain solid gold and silver bands can usually be resized one or two sizes by a jeweller; rings set with stones all around, very wide bands and plated pieces often cannot. Questions people ask

Which finger and which time of day?

Measure the exact finger the ring will live on; the same finger on the other hand is often a half size different. Do it in the evening, at room temperature, when fingers are at their largest. Cold mornings and a hot day after exercise both give a wrong answer.

Can a band be resized?

A plain solid gold or silver band can usually go up or down one or two sizes. Rings with stones all the way round, very wide bands, tension settings and anything plated or gold-filled are hard or impossible to resize. If you are unsure of the size, choose a plain band or ask before ordering.

What if I am between sizes?

Go up for a wide band (6 mm or more), because a wide ring covers more finger and feels tighter. For a slim band, go down if it will be worn over a large knuckle so it does not spin, otherwise pick the size that slides over the knuckle with a little resistance and sits comfortably.
